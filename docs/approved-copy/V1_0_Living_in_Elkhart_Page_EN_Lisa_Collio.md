# Living in Elkhart, Indiana: A Local Agent's Guide to the City

Elkhart sits at the confluence of the St. Joseph and Elkhart Rivers, about 15 miles east of South Bend, and it's the largest city in the Elkhart–Goshen metropolitan area. Lisa Collio works this market every week, and this page is her guide to what actually makes up daily life here — not a tourism brochure, a real one.

## The city at a glance

Elkhart is home to roughly 54,000 people. The county seat is Goshen, just to the south, but Elkhart is the county's largest and most populous city. It sits within the South Bend–Elkhart–Mishawaka region — the area locals call Michiana — and runs on Eastern time. Current mayor Rod Roberson, the city's first Black mayor, took office in 2019 and has centered his administration on infrastructure, public safety, neighborhoods, and quality-of-place investment through an initiative called ASPIRE.

## Downtown Elkhart

Downtown is the cultural center of the city, and it's had a real second act in recent years. It's a compact, closely clustered business district built around the **Lerner Theatre** — originally opened in 1924, reopened in 2011 after an $18 million renovation, and still hosting concerts, comedy, and community events. **Civic Plaza** anchors public gatherings and festivals; the city's downtown social district (DORA) allows visitors to carry drinks between participating businesses; and a monthly **Art Walk** draws people downtown after hours. The **Elkhart Farmers Market** runs weekly. Independent restaurants, coffee shops, and boutiques fill in the rest of the district, and new businesses continue to open there year over year.

## Rivers, parks, and outdoor spaces

**Island Park**, near where the Elkhart River meets the St. Joseph, is the city's signature green space — a pavilion, playground, picnic shelters, open lawn, and direct river access, with paved trails running through it. **Wellfield Botanic Gardens**, a 36-acre "living museum" on North Main Street, holds more than 20 individually themed gardens plus year-round programming and events. The **Riverwalk** connects sections of downtown along the river with public gathering spaces, and during Quilt Gardens season it's part of the region's signature seasonal floral display.

Beyond downtown, **Studebaker Park**, **American Park**, and **McNaughton Park** (home to a splash pad) round out the city's park system, which includes 35 different facilities altogether — ball fields, a public pool, two skate parks, and a downtown ice-skating and roller-blading path that changes with the seasons.

## Museums and the arts

The **RV/MH Hall of Fame & Museum**, tracing the history of recreational vehicles and manufactured housing, has moved to a facility along the toll road — worth knowing if you're picturing it downtown. Closer to the center of the city, the **Ruthmere Museum** and adjoining **Havilah Beardsley House** preserve two historic mansions with period interiors and fine art collections; the **National New York Central Railroad Museum** tells the story of Elkhart's rail history, fitting for a city whose rail yards are still among the largest freight classification yards in the world; and the **Midwest Museum of American Art**, right on Main Street, holds a collection of more than 6,000 works with rotating exhibitions throughout the year. Families with kids often add the **Hall of Heroes Superhero Museum**, a comic and pop-culture museum that's one of only a few of its kind in the country.

## Annual events

The **Elkhart Jazz Festival**, a three-day event on the banks of the Elkhart River each June, is regarded as one of the premier jazz festivals in the country. Quilt Gardens season brings themed floral displays across the region each summer. Downtown hosts a rotating calendar of festivals, concerts, and seasonal celebrations throughout the year, and the Lerner Theatre's own event calendar runs continuously alongside them.

## A brief history

Elkhart was platted in 1832 at the junction of established trails, and its name comes from a Potawatomi word describing an island at the river confluence shaped like an elk's heart. Railroads arrived in the 1850s and shaped the city's early growth; by the late 1800s, musical instrument manufacturing — led by Charles G. Conn — had made Elkhart a national center for band instruments. The first RV factory opened here in 1934, and by 1949 the city had earned its best-known nickname: the RV Capital of the World. Elkhart's downtown commercial district and the State and Division Streets area are both listed on the National Register of Historic Places, and the city was designated a Preserve America Community in 2008.
