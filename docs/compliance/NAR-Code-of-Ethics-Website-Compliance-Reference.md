# NAR Code of Ethics — Website Compliance Reference
**For use by Claude Code when building/editing lisacolliorealtor.com and drafting articles**
Governing standard: National Association of REALTORS® Code of Ethics and Standards of Practice, effective January 1, 2026

> Companion to **IREC-Website-Compliance-Reference.md** (Indiana state law) and **Federal-Fair-Housing-Act-Website-Compliance-Reference.md** (federal FHA). This file only covers what those two don't. **Important distinction:** the Code of Ethics is a **voluntary membership standard** enforced by the Board/Association of REALTORS® through mediation/arbitration and professional-standards proceedings — not a law enforced by courts or the state licensing board. Where the Code sets a *higher* bar than the law (which it does in several places below), Claude Code should build to the Code's stricter standard anyway, since Lisa's membership makes it binding on her. Not legal advice.

---

## 1. Protected Classes Are Broader Than Federal/State Law — Article 10

NAR's Code adds **sexual orientation** and **gender identity** to the protected-class list, on top of race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, and national origin. Neither the federal FHA nor Indiana's IC 22-9.5 currently include these two categories in their statutory text.

**Practical application:** Every rule already established in the Federal FHA reference file (no steering language, no coded "ideal for" phrasing, describe the property not the occupant) now also applies with respect to sexual orientation and gender identity — treat the combined list as: **race, color, religion, sex, disability, familial status, national origin, sexual orientation, gender identity, and (per Indiana law) ancestry.**

---

## 2. No Volunteering Demographic Information — SOP 10-1, 10-2

This is stricter than the FHA's blockbusting rule. When involved in the sale or lease of a residence, REALTORS® **shall not volunteer** information about the racial, religious, or ethnic composition of a neighborhood — even neutrally phrased, even if factually accurate, even without any intent to induce panic selling. (Other demographic information may be provided, but not racial/religious/ethnic composition.)

**Practical application:**
- Neighborhood or market blog content should never characterize an area by its racial, religious, or ethnic makeup — not even in passing, not even framed as a positive ("a wonderfully diverse neighborhood") or as general-interest local color.
- Stick to concrete, non-demographic descriptors: walkability, schools by name (not by "good district" implication tied to demographics), parks, commute times, housing stock, price trends.

---

## 3. Harassment Standard — SOP 10-5

REALTORS® shall not harass any person based on the protected characteristics above, in their real estate business or related activities. Harassment is defined as unwelcome behavior, where the purpose or effect is to create a hostile, abusive, or intimidating environment that limits someone's access to services.

**Practical application:** Relevant mainly if the site ever has comments, reviews, chat widgets, or other user-generated content — moderation guidelines should flag and remove content that could constitute harassment on these grounds, not just profanity or spam.

## 4. Website-Specific Advertising Standards — Article 12, SOPs 12-8 through 12-13

This is the most directly applicable section for Claude Code and goes well beyond the IREC file's broker-name-display rule.

- **SOP 12-8 — Keep it current.** The "true picture" obligation extends to everything displayed on the website. Outdated info (old listings, stale market stats, expired promotions) must be corrected promptly once identified.
- **SOP 12-9 — Firm name and licensure state.** The site must disclose the firm's name **and state(s) of licensure** in a reasonable, readily apparent manner — not just the firm name (which IREC already covers), the licensure state too.
- **SOP 12-10 — No deceptive web practices.** Specifically prohibits: deceptive/unauthorized framing of other brokerages' sites, manipulating third-party content to mislead, deceptive metatags/keywords to divert traffic, using others' content without attribution/permission, and misleading images generally.
- **SOP 12-11 — Data sale/sharing disclosure.** If the site intends to share or sell consumer information gathered online, that must be disclosed in a reasonable, readily apparent way. *(Implemented by the approved Privacy Policy in `Lisa_Collio_Legal_Pages_APPROVED_2026.docx` — "no sale of personal information" and data-sharing practices disclosed visibly; the legacy `disclamers.docx` CCPA language is retired per the Master Plan's document-retirement record, §18–19.<!-- Repo audit note, 31 July 2026, updated same day: this line originally hardcoded "Master Plan v2.4". - **SOP 12-12 — No misleading URLs/domains.** Domain names or URLs used or registered must not present less than a true picture (e.g., a domain implying an affiliation or scope that doesn't exist).
- **SOP 12-13 — Only legitimate designations.** Any professional designations, certifications, or credentials displayed on the site (badges, logos, post-nominal letters) must be ones Lisa is actually and currently entitled to use — no aspirational or expired credentials.

## 5. Honesty and "True Picture" in Communications — Article 12, SOP 12-1

Beyond the general honesty rule already implicit in your brand voice guidelines: REALTORS® must **not represent that brokerage services are free or at no cost** to a client unless there is truly no compensation from any source. If the site ever describes any service as "free," Claude Code should flag this for review rather than publish it by default, since real estate compensation structures rarely meet the literal bar this sets.

---

## 6. Avoiding Exaggeration/Misrepresentation — Article 2

REALTORS® shall avoid exaggeration, misrepresentation, or concealment of pertinent facts about a property or transaction. This overlaps with, but is narrower than, the "no unverifiable superlatives" rule already in the IREC file — Article 2 is specifically about **property and transaction facts** (condition, features, terms), not just marketing claims about Lisa's business performance.

**Practical application:** Property/feature descriptions in any listing-adjacent content should stick to verifiable facts; avoid language that could be read as concealing a known issue or inflating a property's condition or value.

## 7. Quick Pre-Publish Checklist — NAR Layer

Run this in addition to the Indiana and federal checklists already in your other two reference files:

- [ ] Does any neighborhood/market content ever mention racial, religious, or ethnic composition — even neutrally or positively? (Remove — SOP 10-1 bars volunteering it at all.)
- [ ] Does content avoid coded language tied to sexual orientation or gender identity, not just the federally/state-listed classes?
- [ ] Does the site footer/header show the firm's **state(s) of licensure**, not just the firm name?
- [ ] Are all URLs, metatags, and any third-party content properly sourced/attributed, with no deceptive framing?
- [ ] Is any consumer-data-sharing practice disclosed clearly and visibly (not just buried in a long disclaimer)?
- [ ] Are any displayed credentials/designations ones Lisa currently and legitimately holds?
- [ ] Does any "free" service claim actually mean zero compensation from any source? If not, reword.
- [ ] Is site content (listings, stats, promotions) current, with a process to catch and correct stale info?
