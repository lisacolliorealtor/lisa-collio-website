Buying a Home in Elkhart on an RV Industry Job

If you're moving to Elkhart for an RV industry job, a few things about the industry itself will shape your home search more than the usual buyer checklist does.

First: find out which plant, not which company

The Elkhart–Middlebury–Goshen corridor is home to most of the industry's major brands, and plants are spread across a fairly wide radius. Two people working for the "same company" can have commutes that differ by 20 minutes or more depending on which specific plant they're assigned to. Get your plant address before you start looking at neighborhoods, not after.

Second: production pay is documented differently

Many RV plant positions run on piece-rate or production-based pay rather than a flat salary, which means your documented income for mortgage qualification may look different than a straightforward W-2 role. Talk to a lender experienced with RV industry borrowers early — they'll know how to properly document this kind of income for underwriting.

Third: the industry is genuinely cyclical

Elkhart's RV manufacturing strength is also a form of exposure. The city felt this directly during the 2008–2009 downturn, when unemployment briefly spiked into national headlines; the rate has stayed below the national average since 2013, but it's worth building some cushion into your budget if your household income depends entirely on this one sector — a conversation worth having with a financial professional before committing to a mortgage payment.

Fourth: plan your commute before you plan your kitchen

Given how spread out the plants are, driving the actual commute at the actual shift-change time — many plants run early shifts — tells you more than a map ever will. A 12-minute commute on paper can run considerably longer during a 6 a.m. shift change on a two-lane road.
